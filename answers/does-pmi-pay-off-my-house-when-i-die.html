The most common surprise

Does PMI Pay Off My House When I Die?

You pay for it every month. Most homeowners think it protects their family. It protects the bank.

See My Options in 2 Minutes
Short answer: no. Private mortgage insurance (PMI), and the mortgage insurance premium (MIP) on FHA loans, protect the lender when a loan defaults. When you pass away, PMI pays your family nothing and the mortgage is still owed. Coverage that pays your family is a separate life insurance policy that you own.

What PMI really is

Lenders usually require PMI when a buyer puts down less than 20%. State insurance departments say it plainly: PMI protects the lender in case you stop paying. You pay the premium, the bank is the one protected.

Why owning the policy matters

When the policy belongs to you, it pays your family directly. They decide what the money does: pay off the house, keep making payments, cover bills or replace your income. It keeps protecting them when you refinance, sell or move, because it was never tied to the bank.

Questions families ask

Can I cancel PMI and buy mortgage protection instead?

They do different jobs. PMI is a requirement of your loan until you reach the equity your lender needs. Mortgage protection is the coverage that protects your family. Most homeowners with PMI need both for a while.

Is mortgage protection the same as homeowners insurance?

No. Homeowners insurance covers damage to the house. Mortgage protection is life insurance that pays your family when you pass away, and many plans include living benefits for serious illness.

Who pays out when I die and I only have PMI?

Nobody pays your family. The loan is still owed by your spouse or your estate.
